# Client brief — Acme Fermentation Co.

*A fictional brief. Acme Fermentation Co., its people and its numbers are
invented; see `docs/demo/README.md`.*

**Prepared for:** an independent brand and packaging designer
**Prepared by:** Dana Okonjo, co-founder, Acme Fermentation Co.
**Date:** 12 March
**Response wanted by:** 26 March

## 1. Who we are

We are a four-person food business in a converted joinery on the edge of a
mid-sized city. We make small-batch fermented hot sauces and a line of
drinking vinegars. We started in 2021 selling at one Saturday market; we now
supply 34 independent grocers and delis across two counties, we run a webshop
that does about 18% of revenue, and we did EUR 410k last year.

Everything we look like today was made by my co-founder Ruth in an afternoon in
2021: one wordmark set in a free display font, three label templates in a
spreadsheet-driven print tool, and a webshop on a rented theme we have never
touched. It got us here. It will not get us into the next thirty stockists.

## 2. Why we are commissioning work now

Three things happened in the same quarter.

1. A regional grocery group with 11 stores asked us to submit for a shelf
   listing. Their buyer's feedback was blunt: the labels "read as homemade",
   the ingredient panel is hard to scan, and two of our four flavours are
   indistinguishable at 1.5 m.
2. We were copied. A competitor released a range whose bottles are close
   enough to ours that two of our own stockists reordered the wrong product.
   We have no defensible visual asset — nothing we own, nothing distinctive.
3. We are adding a fifth flavour in the autumn and a 3-litre catering format
   in the winter, and our current label system has no room for either. Every
   new SKU means Ruth rebuilding a template by hand.

## 3. What we think we need

We are not designers and we may be wrong about the shape of this. What we
believe we need:

- **A brand identity that survives being scaled down.** Wordmark, a mark that
  works at 12 mm on a bottle neck and on a 3-litre catering drum, and a colour
  system that tells four — soon five — flavours apart from across an aisle.
- **A label system, not five labels.** Something Ruth can extend to a new SKU
  without calling a designer, and that we can hand to our printer without a
  week of back-and-forth. Our printer works in CMYK, needs 3 mm bleed, and has
  told us twice that our current dielines are wrong.
- **Regulatory panels that are actually legible.** Ingredients, allergens,
  net quantity, best-before, and the producer address. This is the part the
  grocery buyer complained about and it is the part we least understand.
- **A webshop refresh that does not become a rebuild.** We want the new
  identity on the existing platform. We do not want a headless rewrite. We do
  not want an app.
- **A short set of rules we can follow ourselves** — what the logo may sit on,
  what it may not, two typefaces, and how to photograph a bottle so it matches
  the rest.

## 4. What we do not want

- A rebrand that throws away the name or the "Acme" wordmark. Our stockists
  order by that name; it stays.
- Anything that needs a subscription to a font we cannot license perpetually
  for print and web.
- Illustration-heavy labels. Our last attempt at that cost EUR 2,800 and
  printed as mud.
- A pitch deck full of mood boards and no dielines. We have had two of those.

## 5. Constraints

- **Budget:** EUR 12,000–15,000 for the whole engagement. This is a hard
  ceiling; it is roughly one month of our gross margin.
- **Timeline:** the grocery group's submission window closes on 30 June.
  Print lead time from our printer is 5 weeks from approved artwork, so
  artwork must be signed off by 20 May at the latest.
- **Us:** Ruth can give about 6 hours a week to this. I can give 3. Neither of
  us can give a full day for a workshop before May.
- **Technical:** labels are pressure-sensitive on a 63 × 95 mm face for the
  250 ml bottles and 90 × 140 mm for the 500 ml. The catering drum spec is not
  fixed yet and we would like the system to absorb it when it is.
- **Legal:** the copied range makes us want something registrable. We have no
  trademark beyond the unregistered name and we would like advice on what is
  worth filing, though we understand that is a lawyer's call, not a
  designer's.

## 6. How we will judge the proposal

We are talking to three people. We will choose on:

1. Whether you have understood that this is a *system* problem, not five
   labels.
2. Whether the plan fits 20 May and EUR 15,000 without either of us pretending.
3. What exactly we get at the end, as files we own, and who can change them.
4. Evidence you have taken something small through to a shelf before.
5. How you would handle the regulatory panel — whose responsibility it is,
   and what you need from us to get it right.

## 7. What we would like back

A written proposal of no more than four pages, covering: your reading of the
problem, the approach and phases, what is delivered at each phase, the
timeline against our 20 May date, the fee and what it does and does not
include, what you need from us and when, and the risks you see. If you think
the budget cannot buy what we have asked for, say so and tell us what you
would cut.
