## 1. Our reading of the problem

This is not a request for five nicer labels. It's a request to replace a
single afternoon's worth of improvised assets with a **system** that can
absorb a fifth flavour, a 3-litre catering format, a demanding regional
buyer, and a copycat competitor — without needing you or Ruth to call a
designer every time.

Three separate pressures are converging on one set of files: shelf
credibility (the buyer's "reads as homemade" feedback), legal/visual
defensibility (the copycat range), and operational capacity (Ruth building
templates by hand). A good label redesign alone solves none of these. What
solves all three is a small set of rules — mark, colour logic, type,
grid — applied consistently enough that a new SKU is a data-entry exercise,
not a design commission.

We also read what you're *not* asking for: no name change, no illustration,
no headless rebuild, no subscription fonts, no deck full of mood boards.
That narrows this to production-grade systems work, which is what we've
priced and scheduled below.

## 2. Approach and phases

### Phase 0 — Working session (not a workshop)
A 90-minute call plus a short written brief-back, sized to the 3–6 hours/week
you have. No all-day session required before May.

### Phase 1 — System foundations *(Weeks 1–2)*
- Wordmark refinement (same name, tightened for reproduction at 12 mm and at
  catering-drum scale)
- A secondary mark for small-format use
- A colour system that separates four (soon five) flavours at aisle distance,
  built from CMYK-stable, print-safe values
- Two-typeface system, licensed for perpetual print + web use
- **Deliverable:** short-form visual direction for your sign-off before we
  build production files. One round of revisions.

### Phase 2 — Label system & regulatory panel *(Weeks 3–5)*
- Master dieline logic for 63×95 mm and 90×140 mm faces, built as a
  parametric template (not five separate files), with a documented rule for
  extending it to the still-unspecified catering drum once that spec exists
- CMYK, 3 mm bleed, built directly against your printer's known requirements
- Regulatory panel layout — ingredients, allergens, net quantity,
  best-before, producer address — designed for legibility at shelf and
  correct hierarchy. **Responsibility split:** we own the panel's design,
  layout, and typographic legibility; you (or your co-packer/regulatory
  adviser) own the accuracy and legal sufficiency of the wording itself. We
  will flag anything that looks legally incomplete, but we are not acting as
  your regulatory counsel. We'll need your current panel copy, and sign-off
  from whoever is accountable for it, by the start of this phase.
- **Deliverable:** print-ready artwork for the four current flavours in both
  bottle sizes, proofed against your printer's spec, plus the editable
  template Ruth will use for flavour five.

### Phase 3 — Webshop application & handover *(Weeks 6–7)*
- New identity applied to your existing theme: header, product imagery
  treatment, colour tokens, no platform change, no rebuild
- A short brand guide (10–14 pages): logo usage and misuse, colour values,
  type, and a one-page photography guide so bottle shots stay consistent
  without hiring a photographer each time
- Handover session with Ruth on using the label template for a new SKU
- Plain-language note on what's worth taking to a trademark lawyer (the
  mark and colour system, most likely), clearly framed as a pointer, not
  legal advice

### What you own at the end
All final artwork, the editable label template, the guide, and source files
(AI/EPS/PDF, fonts licensed for your perpetual use) are yours outright, no
retainer required to use or extend them. Ruth can edit the label template
directly for new SKUs; anything touching the mark itself, we'd ask to review
but you are not contractually required to come back to us.
